Convert Publisher Files to PDF or Word? How to Choose

PubConv · 22 October 2026 · 5 min read

When people move off Publisher, the first question is usually "PDF or Word?" Microsoft itself suggests both, for different reasons. Here's how to decide.

PDF: for keeping documents as they are

PDF freezes the layout. Fonts, pictures, WordArt and columns look the same on any computer, phone or printer, now and in twenty years. Microsoft recommends PDF for keeping viewable copies of Publisher files.

  • Best for archives: old newsletters, bulletins, certificates, programmes.
  • Best for sharing and printing.
  • Not meant for heavy editing (small text fixes are possible in PDF editors).

Word: for documents you'll keep changing

Word is easy to edit, but Publisher and Word think about pages differently. Publisher places boxes freely on a page; Word flows text from top to bottom. Microsoft's own route into Word goes via PDF, and it warns that layouts may shift, especially in graphic-heavy documents. Expect to tidy up.

  • Good for letters, simple flyers and forms you reuse.
  • For posters and signs, Microsoft suggests PowerPoint instead.

The rule of thumb

You want to…Choose
Keep an archive of past documentsPDF
Print or email a document as it wasPDF
Reuse a template every monthPDF for reference, then rebuild in Word or PowerPoint
Make a quick text change to an old flyerPDF plus a PDF editor, or rebuild

Our recommendation

Convert everything to PDF first. It's fast, faithful and future-proof, and it means nothing is at risk. Then rebuild only the handful of templates you actually still use. That's far less work than converting a whole archive to Word and fixing every layout.
