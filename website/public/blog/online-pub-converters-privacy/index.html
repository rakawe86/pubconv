Online .pub Converters: Is It Safe to Upload Your Publisher Files?

PubConv · 24 October 2026 · 5 min read

Search for "convert pub to pdf" and you'll find dozens of websites that do it for free. For a party invitation, that's fine. For a school's archive of newsletters, or a church's membership forms, it's worth pausing first.

What's actually in your .pub files?

Publisher was used for exactly the kind of documents that contain personal information:

  • Newsletters naming pupils, staff and families, often with photos.
  • Member and volunteer lists, rotas and phone trees.
  • Certificates with full names.
  • Forms, sign-up sheets and letters with addresses.

When you upload a file to a website, a copy of all of that goes to someone else's server.

Five questions to ask before uploading

  1. Who runs the service, and where? Is there a real company name and address? Which country are the servers in?
  2. How long are files kept? Look for a clear deletion period in the privacy policy, not just "we respect your privacy".
  3. Are files used for anything else? For example analytics, or improving their software.
  4. Is the connection encrypted? At minimum, the address should start with https.
  5. Does your organisation allow it? Schools, charities and businesses usually have data-protection policies that cover sending personal data to outside services. If you're not sure, ask whoever handles data protection.

This isn't legal advice. Rules such as GDPR in the UK and EU, or FERPA for US schools, may apply to the information in your files. Check with your data-protection lead.

When offline makes more sense

An offline converter runs on your own computer, so files never leave it. That makes it the simpler choice when:

  • the documents contain names, photos or contact details of other people;
  • you have more than a handful of files (most online tools convert one at a time, often with size limits);
  • your internet connection is slow, or files are on a network drive.

Free offline options include LibreOffice, and Publisher itself if you still have it. PubConv is a paid offline converter (with a free trial) that also handles WordArt and newsletter layouts that free tools often break.

The bottom line

Online converters are fine for public documents. For anything with personal information, especially in bulk, converting on your own PC avoids the question entirely.
