Microsoft Publisher End of Support (October 2026): What Happens to Your .pub Files

PubConv · 27 October 2026 · 6 min read

Microsoft has retired Publisher. If your school, church, charity or office has years of newsletters, flyers and certificates saved as .pub files, now is the time to make sure you can still open them. Here's what changed and what to do, including the free options.

What exactly changed?

According to Microsoft's announcement:

  • Microsoft 365 subscribers lose access to Publisher from 1 October 2026, as an update removes it. They can no longer open or edit .pub files in Publisher, and can't download or install it.
  • One-time-purchase versions (such as Office 2021 or LTSC 2021) keep working, but support ended on 13 October 2026, so there are no more security updates or fixes.
  • Microsoft recommends converting your files to PDF to keep a viewable copy, or to PDF and then into Word if you need to edit them. It warns that layouts may shift in Word, especially in graphic-heavy documents.

Your .pub files don't disappear. They stay where they are. What disappears for most people is the program that can open them properly.

Who is affected?

Anyone whose .pub files are their only copy of a document. Typically:

  • Schools: newsletters, certificates, programmes and signage templates going back years.
  • Churches and community groups: weekly bulletins, event flyers and membership forms.
  • Small businesses: brochures, menus, price lists and business cards.
  • Families: invitations, calendars and family newsletters.

Your options, from free to effortless

1. Save as PDF in Publisher (free, if you still have it)

If any PC still has Publisher, open each file and choose File → Save As → PDF. It's perfect quality, but it takes one file at a time. Microsoft also publishes a sample PowerShell script that does this in bulk, but it automates Publisher itself, so it only works on a PC that still has Publisher installed.

2. Free converters such as LibreOffice (free, mixed quality)

LibreOffice Draw can open many .pub files. Simple documents often look fine. In our tests, though, WordArt headlines disappeared or turned into black shapes, and newsletter articles that flow between linked text boxes were printed twice and ran off the page. See the test results.

3. Online converters (free, but you upload your files)

Many websites convert .pub files. They're convenient for a few files, but you're uploading documents that may contain names, addresses and other personal details. What to check before uploading.

4. A dedicated offline converter

Desktop tools convert whole folders on your own PC. PubConv is one of them: it converts entire folder trees to PDF offline, and was built to get WordArt and linked newsletter text right. There's a free trial.

A simple plan for an organisation

  1. Find the files. Search for *.pub on shared drives, OneDrive, SharePoint and staff PCs.
  2. Decide what to keep. Most old newsletters only need a PDF copy. Templates you still use can be rebuilt in Word or PowerPoint.
  3. Convert in bulk to PDF, keeping the folder structure.
  4. Spot-check a few PDFs from each year, especially ones with WordArt or multi-page newsletters.
  5. Keep the originals alongside the PDFs. Disk space is cheap; lost documents aren't.

Bottom line

Nothing is lost yet, but the tools that open .pub files properly are going away. Convert your archive to PDF while it's easy, check the documents that matter most, and keep the originals.
