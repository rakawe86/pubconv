PubConv vs LibreOffice: We Converted 33 Publisher Files with Both

PubConv · 25 October 2026 · 7 min read

LibreOffice is the go-to free option for opening Publisher files, and it's good software. We wanted a fair, concrete answer to one question: where does it fall short, and does PubConv actually do better there? So we made a test set and converted it with both.

How we tested

  • We created 33 documents in Microsoft Publisher: 23 that each exercise one feature (fonts, tables, images, rotation, WordArt, linked text boxes, Arabic, master pages and so on) and 10 realistic documents (flyer, newsletter, menu, certificate, church bulletin, brochure, calendar, poster, sign-up form and an Arabic flyer).
  • For each one, Publisher exported its own PDF as the reference.
  • We converted the .pub files with LibreOffice and with PubConv on the same PC, and compared every page with Publisher's version by eye and with automated text checks.
  • We also made 39 WordArt test files covering Publisher's WordArt styles.

Caveat: these are documents we made. Real-world archives contain things we didn't test, so try both tools on your own files.

The results at a glance

WordArt: the most visible difference

WordArt is the curved, slanted or wavy headline text that makes Publisher documents look like Publisher documents. LibreOffice reads the WordArt shape but not its text, so it draws the empty outline. On our certificate that became two thick black curves where the title should be.

Newsletters: text that flows between boxes

In a newsletter, an article often starts in one text box and continues in another, sometimes on the next page. Publisher doesn't store where the text breaks; it works it out each time it opens the file. LibreOffice gives the whole article to every box, so it appears twice and overflows.

PubConv flows the text itself using Publisher's rules: margins, line spacing, paragraph spacing and hyphenation. In our tests every line matched Publisher's, including the hyphens.

Where PubConv isn't perfect

  • Rainbow and multi-colour gradient WordArt is drawn in one colour, and 3D WordArt effects are drawn flat.
  • For everything other than WordArt and linked text, PubConv uses the same open-source layout engine as LibreOffice, so it shares that engine's strengths and limits.

Verdict

For simple documents, LibreOffice is a fine free choice. If your files have WordArt headlines or newsletters (as most school, church and community documents do), PubConv's output is much closer to what you designed in Publisher. Try both on your own files; PubConv's trial is free and works at full quality.
